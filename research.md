My research interests are in high-dimensional statistics, with applications in the physical sciences. So far my work has been in number theory, dynamical systems, and mathematical biology.

<div class="entry">
  <h3>Integer Sequences from the Cantor Set</h3>
  <p class="entry-meta">Senior Thesis · Pomona College · Advisor: Dr. Taylor McAdam · Jan. 2025 – May 2026</p>
  <ul>
    <li>Defined and proved the existence of a new infinite integer sequence derived from the reduced fraction-valued endpoints of the Cantor set.</li>
    <li>Studied secondary sequences built from those endpoints using combinatorial, number-theoretic, and computational tools.</li>
    <li>Generalized the results to other Cantor-type constructions, including middle-half, fat, Smith–Volterra, and stochastic variants.</li>
    <li>Published the sequences to the <a href="https://oeis.org/A390119">Online Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences (A390119)</a>. A paper with the theoretical results is in preparation.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<div class="entry">
  <h3>Rock–Paper–Scissors Population Dynamics</h3>
  <p class="entry-meta">Summer Undergraduate Researcher · NSF-Simons National Institute for Theory and Mathematics in Biology · June 2025 – Aug. 2025</p>
  <p class="muted">Advisors: Dr. Alvin Bayliss and Dr. Vladimir Volpert, Northwestern University</p>
  <ul>
    <li>Modeled networks of rock–paper–scissors systems to study how coupling strength affects stability, coexistence, and pattern formation.</li>
    <li>Simulated three-species dynamics with nonlinear differential equations and custom Python implementations of the Improved Euler method.</li>
    <li>Used high-performance computing and spectral analysis to identify synchronized, desynchronized, intermittent, quasi-periodic, and chaotic regimes.</li>
    <li>Presented the findings at the inaugural NITMB MathBio Convergence Conference.</li>
  </ul>
</div>
